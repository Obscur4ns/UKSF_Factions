## 1. Purpose

This document defines the initial research and design direction for the first faction in `UKSF_Factions`.

The faction is intended to serve as the principal hostile force for a contemporary UKSF deployment to **North Takistan**. It should evoke the atmosphere and threat identity of a present-day ISIS-K-style organisation while remaining fully fictional and compatible with established Armaverse lore.

This is a **design reference**, not a configuration file. No equipment class names, mod dependencies, group compositions, or final artwork are locked by this document unless explicitly marked as a decision.

The faction must be:

- recognisably different from the legacy Takistani Army and Takistani Militia;
- suitable for rural, mountain, village, town, and urban scenarios;
- believable as a contemporary insurgent organisation;
- visually coherent without making every fighter identical;
- capable of supporting low-end insurgent encounters and higher-threat specialist elements;
- lore-safe for public release;
- flexible enough for mission makers to scale capability without creating separate factions.

---

## 2. Project Continuity

### 2.1 Armaverse basis

Takistan is an established fictional Central Asian state in the Green Sea region.

The events of *Arma 2: Operation Arrowhead* ended with the Takistani military regime being defeated by coalition forces. Official *British Armed Forces* material then established that a major guerrilla insurgency erupted shortly afterwards and that British forces deployed into Takistan for a large counter-insurgency operation.

Official material also establishes cross-border activity involving **Karzeghistan**, giving the wider Takistani border region a canonical basis for persistent irregular warfare.

This provides a strong foundation for a later extremist movement without rewriting the original setting.

### 2.2 UKSF_Factions continuity assumption

`UKSF_Factions` should use a **contemporary Armaverse continuity**:

- Armaverse countries, geography, wars and historical events remain valid background.
- Equipment and force presentation are based primarily on the contemporary 2020s rather than strictly reproducing Arma 3's 2035 equipment aesthetic.
- The current project reference year is approximately **2026**.
- Exact dates between older Armaverse events and the current UKSF continuity should remain deliberately loose unless needed for campaign writing.

This allows Takistan to retain its established history while supporting equipment and visual references appropriate to the unit's current-day setting.

## 3. Real-World Research Reference

### 3.1 Primary analogue

The principal real-world reference for NTEF is **ISIS-K / ISIL-K**, specifically its role as a hardline transnational extremist organisation operating in Afghanistan and the wider Central/South Asian region.

United Nations material records that ISIL-K was established in 2015 from former militant commanders in the Afghanistan/Pakistan environment.

Current official Western and United Nations assessments continue to describe ISIS-K as a significant threat beyond Afghanistan. The United States National Counterterrorism Center's 2026 public profile describes it as one of ISIS's most lethal branches and notes activity or plotting extending beyond its original operating area.

### 3.2 What should transfer into the fictional faction

The following characteristics are useful design references:

- a transnational rather than purely nationalist identity;
- rejection of the existing state and its institutions;
- hostility toward rival armed movements as well as government and foreign forces;
- a strong and recognisable organisational identity;
- a mixture of local recruits and more committed ideological cadres;
- a capability spectrum ranging from lightly equipped local members to better-equipped core fighters;
- use of captured, diverted, commercially available and regionally common equipment rather than a standard state-issued inventory;
- a willingness to operate across political and geographic boundaries;
- continued relevance despite sustained counter-terrorism pressure.

### 3.3 What must not transfer

`UKSF_Factions` should **not** reproduce:

- the ISIS/ISIL name;
- ISIS-K or ISKP branding;
- real flags or seals;
- real religious slogans or declarations;
- real extremist media products;
- real leaders or personalities;
- real propaganda text, audio, chants or songs;
- real recruitment material;
- real attack instructions, procedures or technical material;
- one-to-one recreations of documented terrorist incidents.

The intent is to reproduce a believable **fictional threat archetype**, not a real organisation.

## 4. Fictional Organisation

## 4.1 Working name

**Northern Takistan Emirate Front**

**Acronym:** NTEF

This is currently treated as the English-language/NATO intelligence designation.

A local self-designation is intentionally **TBD**. It should not be invented until a consistent Takistani naming and language convention has been established for the wider mod.

### Naming goals

The final name should:

- sound plausible within Takistan;
- remain fictional;
- avoid direct reuse of real organisation names;
- avoid unnecessary similarity to ISIS/ISIL naming structures;
- be easy for mission makers and players to recognise;
- provide a short editor-friendly acronym.

---

## 4.2 Proposed origin

NTEF emerges from the instability that followed the defeat of the old Takistani regime and the subsequent insurgency.

The movement is **not** a direct continuation of the original Takistani Militia.

Instead, it develops years later from a convergence of:

- extremist splinter elements from older insurgent organisations;
- radicalised members of former Takistani armed groups;
- younger recruits with no direct connection to the original war;
- foreign volunteers and cross-border militants;
- defectors from rival armed movements;
- criminal or smuggling networks used for access and sustainment.

The result is a movement that considers older Takistani nationalist and tribal insurgents insufficiently committed to its ideological project.

This gives NTEF a believable reason to fight **both the Takistani state and other insurgent factions**.

---

## 4.3 Political and ideological character

NTEF is a **hardline transnational jihadist movement** seeking to replace existing secular and traditional political structures with its own extremist religious government.

The faction should be portrayed as:

- authoritarian;
- absolutist;
- hostile to political compromise;
- hostile to foreign military presence;
- hostile to the Takistani government;
- hostile to rival insurgent groups that reject its authority;
- coercive toward local communities;
- willing to exploit instability and weak governance;
- interested in expanding influence beyond North Takistan.

The mod should not attempt to reproduce or explain real-world extremist theology in detail. Fictional lore only needs enough ideological context to explain relationships, appearance and mission scenarios.

## 5. Area of Activity

### Primary area

**North Takistan**

The terrain supports the faction's intended mixture of:

- isolated settlements;
- mountain valleys;
- agricultural areas;
- road networks;
- small towns;
- larger population centres;
- remote border approaches.

### Secondary lore area

The organisation may be described as having influence or support networks extending toward:

- other regions of Takistan;
- the Karzeghistani border;
- remote Green Sea region transit routes.

Exact bases, routes and territorial boundaries should remain campaign-specific rather than hardcoded into faction lore.

## 6. Relationship to Other Forces

### Takistani government

**Hostile**

NTEF rejects the legitimacy of the current Takistani state and treats government security forces as a principal enemy.

### UK / NATO / coalition forces

**Hostile**

Foreign military presence is used by the movement as part of its justification for continued conflict.

### Legacy Takistani insurgents

**Hostile or competitive**

Traditional nationalist, tribal or locally focused insurgents may oppose the government while simultaneously opposing NTEF.

This is important for future faction design because it allows:

- three-way conflicts;
- temporary local arrangements;
- rival insurgent missions;
- intelligence-led scenarios involving competing armed networks.

### Criminal networks

**Transactional**

Smugglers and organised criminal groups may interact with NTEF without sharing its ideology.

### Civilians

**Not a faction-level enemy**

Civilian relationships should be represented through mission design rather than by making civilians mechanically hostile.

NTEF may intimidate, coerce or attempt to control civilian communities, but civilian behaviour should remain scenario-dependent.

## 7. Visual Identity

## 7.1 Overall visual target

NTEF should be immediately identifiable as a **contemporary extremist insurgent faction**, but should not become a caricature where every fighter wears black clothing and a face covering.

The intended visual character is:

- austere;
- low-colour;
- regionally grounded;
- irregular;
- increasingly coherent as unit capability rises.

### Primary palette

- black;
- charcoal;
- dark olive;
- faded green;
- tan;
- sand;
- brown;
- off-white;
- muted civilian colours.

Black should be an **identity accent**, not the universal uniform colour.

---

## 7.2 Clothing

### Local / low-tier fighters

Possible appearance:

- traditional loose shirts and trousers;
- civilian shirts;
- older field jackets;
- mixed civilian trousers;
- sandals;
- trainers;
- military boots;
- scarves;
- pakol-style or regional headwear;
- simple chest rigs;
- older webbing.

These fighters should look locally sourced and individually equipped.

### Core fighters

Possible appearance:

- more consistent earth-tone clothing;
- mixed surplus camouflage;
- better chest rigs;
- simple plate carriers;
- military boots;
- gloves;
- scarves or face coverings;
- occasional captured helmets.

They should still appear irregular but noticeably better equipped.

### Assault / veteran cadre

Possible appearance:

- darker and more coordinated clothing;
- modern plate carriers;
- higher-quality load-bearing equipment;
- better footwear;
- helmets in limited numbers;
- eye protection;
- weapon optics;
- radios;
- limited night-vision equipment.

These units provide the faction with a contemporary high-threat element without making advanced equipment universal.

---

## 7.3 Headwear and face coverings

Headwear should be varied.

Potential categories:

- pakol-type hats;
- scarves;
- wrapped head coverings;
- knit caps;
- patrol caps;
- bare head;
- captured military helmets;
- modern commercial helmets for select high-tier units.

Face coverings should be common enough to contribute to the faction identity but not mandatory on every unit.

---

## 8. Insignia and Branding

NTEF needs a **completely fictional visual identity**.

### Design principles

The emblem should:

- be simple enough to recognise on a flag, patch and vehicle;
- work in monochrome;
- avoid real extremist religious text;
- avoid reproducing the ISIS seal;
- avoid copying the ISIS black-banner layout;
- avoid real national or religious insignia;
- remain visually severe and easily readable.

### Proposed visual direction

A future first-pass emblem could combine:

- a circular or angular outer device;
- a stylised three-peak mountain motif representing North Takistan;
- a simple central geometric mark;
- a limited black / off-white / muted-earth palette.

The first artwork iteration should contain **no Arabic or pseudo-Arabic text**.

Text can be revisited only after the fictional language and naming conventions are settled.

### Required future artwork

Eventually generate:

- faction flag;
- shoulder/arm patch;
- editor faction icon;
- vehicle stencil;
- map/intelligence icon;
- optional graffiti or environmental marking;
- optional intelligence dossier branding.

Artwork development is a separate task and should be performed after the faction identity is approved.

## 9. Equipment Philosophy

NTEF should not have a perfectly standardised arsenal.

Its equipment should appear to come from four broad sources:

1. regional legacy weapons;
2. captured Takistani government equipment;
3. black-market or smuggled equipment;
4. limited commercial or foreign equipment.

The result should be **controlled variety**, not random equipment generation.

---

## 9.1 Rifles

Primary visual family:

- AKM-pattern rifles;
- AK-74-pattern rifles;
- AK-100-series-style rifles;
- compact AK-pattern carbines.

Older weapons should dominate lower-tier units.

Newer AK variants and captured modern rifles should become more common among veteran and specialist units.

### Captured Western weapons

Western-pattern rifles may appear in **small numbers** among experienced or high-status fighters.

They should remain unusual enough that finding one visually implies capture, access or status.

---

## 9.2 Machine guns

Likely families:

- PK / PKM;
- RPK-pattern weapons;
- occasional captured light machine guns.

PK-pattern machine guns should provide the faction's principal recognisable general-purpose machine-gun identity.

---

## 9.3 Precision weapons

Likely families:

- SVD-pattern rifles;
- older scoped rifles;
- limited captured modern marksman rifles.

Avoid giving every squad a highly equipped precision shooter.

---

## 9.4 Anti-armour weapons

The visual baseline should be built around older reusable or disposable regional anti-armour weapons, particularly RPG-pattern systems.

Modern guided weapons should **not** be part of the default faction identity unless later campaign research specifically justifies them.

---

## 9.5 Sidearms

Sidearms should be uncommon outside:

- leaders;
- vehicle crews;
- specialists;
- veteran fighters.

Possible visual families include older Soviet-pattern pistols and captured modern handguns.

---

## 9.6 Optics and accessories

### Low tier

- iron sights;
- minimal weapon accessories.

### Core tier

- occasional red-dot or low-power optic;
- weapon lights where assets allow;
- limited modern furniture.

### Veteran / specialist tier

- modern optics;
- selected suppressors where appropriate;
- better weapon accessories;
- night-compatible equipment in small numbers.

The faction should never look like a Western special operations force wearing insurgent clothing.

---

## 9.7 Communications

Communications equipment should visually reinforce hierarchy.

- Local fighters: little or no visible radio equipment.
- Core fighters: basic handheld radios.
- Leaders / specialists: more capable commercial or captured radios.
- Veteran assault elements: reliable individual or team communications where available.

Exact ACRE radio distribution will be decided during configuration work.

---

## 9.8 Night capability

Night capability should be deliberately uneven.

Most fighters should **not** have NVGs.

Possible distribution:

- local fighters: none;
- core fighters: normally none;
- leaders / scouts / veteran assault personnel: occasional NVGs;
- specialist cells: limited modern night capability.

Thermal devices should be significantly rarer than conventional NVGs.

This allows night missions to retain a meaningful technological advantage for UKSF while preventing the faction from becoming helpless after dark.

---

## 9.9 Commercial technology

Contemporary non-state armed organisations increasingly exploit commercially available technology.

For gameplay and visual authenticity, NTEF may eventually include limited roles associated with:

- commercial quadcopters;
- civilian cameras;
- rugged smartphones/tablets;
- commercial radios.

Any drone implementation should focus on **mission capability and atmosphere**, not recreation of real terrorist procedures.

---

## 10. Vehicle Philosophy

The default NTEF vehicle pool should prioritise civilian and light utility vehicles.

### Core vehicle categories

- civilian pickup trucks;
- unarmed pickups;
- armed technical variants;
- civilian SUVs;
- vans;
- older utility vehicles;
- motorcycles where suitable assets exist;
- light cargo trucks.

### Captured vehicles

A small number of captured government vehicles may appear.

Possible categories:

- military utility vehicles;
- police vehicles;
- military cargo trucks;
- light protected vehicles.

### Heavy armour

**No tanks or infantry fighting vehicles should be part of the baseline faction.**

Mission makers can introduce captured armour manually when required by a specific scenario.

This prevents NTEF from drifting into a conventional army faction.

---

## 11. Unit Capability Tiers

The faction should use a consistent capability ladder.

### Tier 1 — Local Fighter

Purpose:

- most common infantry;
- visual mass;
- village and rural presence.

Characteristics:

- civilian/traditional clothing;
- basic chest rig or webbing;
- older rifle;
- little specialist equipment;
- no NVGs.

### Tier 2 — Core Fighter

Purpose:

- standard organised combatant.

Characteristics:

- more coherent clothing;
- better load-bearing equipment;
- wider weapon selection;
- occasional armour;
- occasional radio;
- limited optics.

### Tier 3 — Veteran / Assault Fighter

Purpose:

- higher-threat infantry;
- raid/HVT protection;
- difficult mission encounters.

Characteristics:

- better armour;
- better weapons;
- optics;
- radios;
- occasional helmet;
- limited night capability.

### Tier 4 — Specialist

Potential roles:

- machine gunner;
- marksman;
- anti-armour gunner;
- medic;
- vehicle crew;
- drone operator;
- communications specialist.

Specialist roles should inherit the visual quality of the relevant capability tier rather than receiving a universal "special forces" appearance.

### Tier 5 — Leadership / Security

Purpose:

- HVT missions;
- command elements;
- bodyguard groups;
- intelligence scenarios.

Characteristics:

- visually distinctive but not theatrical;
- good equipment access;
- sidearm;
- radio;
- small security detail.

No real extremist personalities should be recreated.

## 15. Mission-Making Identity

NTEF should support scenarios involving:

- counter-insurgency;
- rural security operations;
- urban operations;
- intelligence-led raids;
- HVT capture scenarios;
- protection of local government forces;
- rivalry between armed factions;
- convoy/security missions;
- hostage or civilian-protection scenarios;
- night operations;
- border-region operations;
- training scenarios representing a contemporary high-threat insurgent force.

The faction should be capable of presenting a credible threat without requiring mission makers to give every unit advanced equipment.

---

## 16. Distinction From Future Takistani Factions

NTEF should not become the only irregular Takistani faction in the mod.

Future development should preserve room for at least:

### Traditional Takistani insurgents

Identity:

- nationalist;
- tribal/local;
- pragmatic;
- more closely related to the original post-Operation Arrowhead insurgency.

### Regional proxy militia

Identity:

- externally supported;
- better supplied;
- more military organisation;
- more uniform equipment.

### NTEF

Identity:

- extremist;
- transnational;
- ideological;
- hostile to rival insurgents;
- stronger visual branding;
- capability concentrated in a smaller veteran cadre.

This separation will make multi-faction campaigns substantially more believable.

## 22. Current Working Decision

For initial `UKSF_Factions` development, the North Takistan deployment enemy is provisionally defined as:

> **Northern Takistan Emirate Front (NTEF)** — a fictional, hardline transnational extremist movement that emerged from the long-running instability following the collapse of the old Takistani regime. It combines locally recruited insurgents with a smaller, better-equipped veteran cadre and operates principally in North Takistan while maintaining a wider cross-border outlook.

This definition is sufficient to begin the **asset and dependency audit**, but faction name, emblem and exact equipment remain subject to approval before configuration work begins.
