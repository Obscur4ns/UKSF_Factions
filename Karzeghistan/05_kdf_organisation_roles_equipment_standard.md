# 1. Purpose

This document defines the practical Arma organisation, roles and equipment identity of the **Karzeghistani Defence Forces**.

It establishes:

- overall force character;
- capability tiers;
- infantry roles;
- editor catalogue;
- weapon distribution;
- body armour;
- helmets;
- optics;
- communications;
- night capability;
- unmanned systems;
- vehicle philosophy;
- mechanised capability;
- aviation;
- coastal capability;
- artillery and air defence;
- group templates;
- first-release scope;
- visual distinctions from TNA and SPF.

The central design rule is:

> **KDF is a small, professional regional defence force with meaningful Western/NATO interoperability, but it remains Karzeghistani rather than becoming a generic NATO army.**

---

# PART I — STRATEGIC ROLE

## 2. National Mission

KDF exists primarily for:

- territorial defence;
- protection of Karzeghistani sovereignty;
- defence of Sharig/Shapur;
- reinforcement of the Karzeghistani Border Guard;
- protection of strategic infrastructure;
- response to armed proxy or militant activity;
- limited international/partner exercises.

It is not designed around expeditionary global warfare.

---

## 3. Historical Security Context

Karzeghistan had meaningful interaction with NATO forces before and during the 2012 Takistani crisis.

Official Armaverse material places British/NATO activity around the Karzeghistani border and Shapur e-Dalanper.

This gives the project a strong reason for a 2026 KDF that has absorbed:

- Western training;
- interoperability procedures;
- communications standards;
- selected donor equipment;

without requiring Karzeghistan to be a NATO member.

---

# PART II — NATO-PARTNER DESIGN REFERENCE

## 4. Interoperability, Not Cloning

Modern NATO partner programmes provide a useful structural reference.

NATO describes partner interoperability in terms of compatible:

- standards;
- procedures;
- command and control;
- training;
- logistics;
- equipment.

NATO capacity-building programmes also emphasise:

- viable institutions;
- sustainable capabilities;
- local ownership;
- tailored force development.

For KDF this means:

> Western assistance should be most visible in organisation, communications, training, logistics and selected equipment—not in replacing every regional weapon and vehicle with a NATO equivalent.

## 6. Relative Capability

KDF should not contain the same huge quality gap seen across TNA.

Its average soldier is better equipped than a TNA Regional Infantry soldier.

However, KDF still contains:

- ordinary line infantry;
- older equipment;
- reserve/second-line capability.

# PART IV — CAPABILITY LAYERS

## 7. Three-Layer Model

KDF uses three practical capability layers.

### Tier A — Regular Defence Force

Normal infantry and mechanised force.

### Tier B — Rapid Response

Higher-readiness, partner-trained force for Sharig and national contingencies.

### Tier C — Special Operations

Very small specialist element.

These are `UKSF_Factions` implementation categories, not claimed canonical Karzeghistani TO&E.

---

Regular KDF represents the majority of deployable ground forces.

- territorial defence;

- infrastructure security;
- conventional infantry operations;
